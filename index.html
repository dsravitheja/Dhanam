About Dhanam
What this is

Dhanam is a personal financial calculator for Indian users — property cost and home-loan planning, company car lease tax analysis, car loan and depreciation, and SIP (mutual fund) investment planning. It runs entirely in this browser tab as a static page: nothing is installed on a server, there's no company behind it processing your numbers, and every figure on screen is a live calculation from whatever you type in.

This is not financial, tax, or investment advice. It's a calculator for exploring scenarios with your own numbers — it doesn't know your full financial picture, and nothing on this site should be read as a recommendation of what to do with your money.

Privacy

Nothing you type is ever sent anywhere. This is checkable, not just claimed: open your browser's developer tools, switch to the Network tab, and use the app — you'll see no request leave this page. (The one exception is the app's own offline-caching service worker asking this same site for its own files, so the app still works with no signal — that's the app loading itself, not sending anything about you.)

  • No accounts, no login, no password.
  • No bank or broker connections — every figure is hand-typed by you.
  • No sync between devices. A backup file you download and re-import yourself is the only way data moves between them.
  • No analytics, no tracking pixels, no error-reporting service.
  • No recommendation engine — nothing ranks or suggests a "best" financial product for you.

Anything you choose to save (a Dhanam Worth balance sheet, a Compare Cars shortlist, a "remembered" loan input) is stored in this browser's local storage, on this device only. Download Backup, Restore Backup and Erase My Data (in Dhanam Worth) are the only ways data leaves, re-enters, or is deleted — there is no other channel.

Known gaps
  • Old regime in Dhanam Car: standard deduction and 80C deduction only; HRA and 80D are not modelled.
  • Dhanam Car assumes a salary carve-out — the car's cost comes from your CTC, reducing take-home. If an employer instead adds the car on top of your CTC, you stay on your current salary and are taxed only on the perquisite value; this case is not modelled.
  • Payslip EPF amount: used as-is in both scenarios (Baseline and Carve-out), since a carve-out comes from allowances rather than Basic salary.
  • Home loan "What if I invested instead?" (Buy-vs-SIP): prepayment earnings equal the loan's interest rate (certain, untaxed); SIP returns are not guaranteed, and gains are taxed on sale. The comparison omits the loan's own tax benefits (Section 24 interest deduction, 80C principal deduction in old regime), so it's a rough sketch, not a verdict.
  • Home loan "What if I prepay?" net-worth impact: baseline assumes Dhanam Worth's own debt-payoff projection (all liabilities, not this loan specifically); the plan's interest-saving accrues through its payoff year, not by the horizon year; and the plan's yearly cost is not counted, so the net-worth figure is an upper bound.
  • Every corpus/gain figure in Dhanam Grow, and every SIP-side figure in Dhanam Home's Buy-vs-SIP and prepay-vs-SIP comparisons, is pre-tax. Building a correct post-tax version needs per-installment holding-period tracking that the current SIP engine doesn't keep — a real restructure, not a quick add — and a precisely-wrong post-tax number would be worse than an honest, stated gap. For now this is disclosed rather than modelled; see the caveat above the Buy-vs-SIP hero for the short version.
  • Dhanam Grow's flat monthly SIP calculation and its step-up SIP calculation disagree by about 1%, even at 0% step-up, because they use different interest-crediting conventions internally (one compounds like an annuity-due, the other month-by-month like an ordinary annuity). It's a known, measured gap, not a bug we haven't found — and it's why the app never plots those two as two lines on the same chart.
